The site served 2.4× the visitors it served the year before. That is not a seasonal bump — it is a level change that happened in July 2025 and has held for thirteen straight months.

The shape of the recovery

Monthly visitors · Mar 2023 – Aug 2026

The averages tell it cleanly. Through the flat period the site averaged 4,023 visitors a month. Over the last twelve months it averaged 11,992 — three times as many. August 2026 is tracking to roughly 10,600, its normal level for the month.

Worth chasing down: nothing in the site's own change log explains the February 2024 drop — no republish, no domain change, no pages deleted that month. The likeliest causes sit outside the site: a search ranking shift, or a change to analytics or tag setup. Twenty-three further crawlers make up the remainder. The site already does the basics right: robots.txt explicitly permits AI reading, and a machine-readable llms.txt summary is live — which is a large part of why ChatGPT reads this site as often as it does.

The gap is what those crawlers find once they arrive. There is no structured data on the homepage — no organisation record, no brand list, no contact block, and no article markup on the fifty-plus nutrition and news stories. AI systems are reading San Miguel Foods in prose and inferring the facts. Companies that publish structured data get quoted with their facts intact.

What is leaking

Four findings
Fix

614 leads arrived and none of them left a record

The platform counts 614 leads for the year, but the site's form records are empty — every one of those leads is a phone tap or an email click that goes straight to a mailbox, untracked and unrouted. Business Opportunities carries six programmes: Chick-n-Juicy franchise, three reseller programmes, broiler contract growing and cassava assembly. Not one has a form that records who asked, for what, and whether anyone replied.

Platform form submissions: 0 records · leads reported: 614 · conversion 0.42%
Fix

An "Under Construction" page is live, indexable and listed for AI

www.sanmiguelfoods.com/under-construction returns a normal page, carries no no-index instruction, sits in the public sitemap and is named in llms.txt. Google can serve it and ChatGPT can cite it. It is a two-minute fix.

HTTP 200 · in sitemap.xml · in llms.txt · no robots meta tag
Watch

Visitors read 1.68 pages and stop

Across 147,421 visits the site averages under two pages. There are 88 pages live, roughly fifty of them substantial nutrition and corporate stories, and almost nobody reaches a second one. This is a navigation and internal-linking problem, and it is worst on mobile, where nearly half the audience is.

What we would do next

In order

  1. Put a real inquiry form on every business opportunity

    Six programmes, one form each, routed to the right desk with an automatic acknowledgement to the sender. Turns 614 anonymous taps a year into a named, trackable pipeline the business development team can work.

  2. Clear the under-construction page and tighten the sitemap

    No-index or remove it, then re-submit. Quick, and it stops a placeholder from representing the brand in search results and in AI answers.

  3. Add structured data across the site

    Organisation and contact markup on the homepage, brand records on the brand pages, article markup on the fifty-plus stories. This is what turns "an AI read the page" into "an AI quoted San Miguel Foods correctly". At 50,092 crawler visits a year, the leverage is immediate.

  4. Fix the mobile reading path

    Related-story links, a clear route from any landing page into Brands and Business Opportunities, and a menu that works with one thumb. Moving 1.68 pages per visit to 2.2 is roughly 79,000 additional page views a year at current traffic.

  5. Give Exports its own front door

    One in five visitors is overseas and they read the fewest pages of anyone. A dedicated export and diaspora landing page, linked from the homepage, serves an audience the site already earns and currently loses.

  6. Hold a monthly publishing rhythm

    One nutrition or corporate story a month, published on schedule. The content is already good; it needs to keep arriving so search engines and AI systems have a reason to keep coming back.
