Is deal flow the binding constraint in a brokerage?

A five-lens synthesis: practitioner, academic, skeptic, economist, and historian. Every claim independently checked against its primary source before publication.

01

The 60-Second Summary

All five lenses reject the proposition, and the peer-reviewed tier rejects it on theory as well as evidence: under a fixed percentage commission with free entry, deal flow cannot be the binding constraint at equilibrium, because entry competes flow away until per-agent profit reaches zero. Hsieh and Moretti (JPE, 2003) find that when house prices rise, more brokers enter, houses sold per agent falls, and the typical agent’s real wage does not move. Barwick and Pathak (RAND, 2015) find agent entry raises neither sale probability nor speed. What binds a brokerage is whatever about it cannot be replicated by the next entrant — the relationship, the information, or the closed side of the market — and volume is the visible symptom of holding or losing that. The contested part is what to do about it: the practitioner and economist lenses put conversion capacity and working-capital float ahead of access, and those operate on a shorter clock. The one claim that should not be repeated is that a dead month is structural; the evidence points the other way, and saying so converts a measurable conversion failure into an alibi.

Five Key Findings, Ranked by Reliability

Free entry dissipates the rent: more agents, less output each, unchanged wages
1
Reliability: Peer-reviewed, published
9/10

Across US cities, when land prices rise the broker share of the workforce rises, houses sold per agent falls, and the typical agent’s real wage is roughly constant. The extra commission pool is competed away in entry rather than converted into more transactions. Barwick and Pathak add that early-2000s house-price appreciation explains 24% of entry and a 31% fall in houses sold per agent. Verified caveat: both are correlational or structural, not experimental — Hsieh and Moretti is a cross-city design.

Intermediaries keep the segments where matching needs judgment
5
Reliability: Industry report on AM Best data; historical cases documented
7/10

US independent agencies wrote 39.0% of personal-lines premium in 2024 but 87.2% of commercial lines. The channel lost the half a quote engine can handle and kept the half that still requires a person to assemble the match. The historical pattern runs the same way: Lloyd’s underwriting grew around a shipping-intelligence business decades before the Society formalised in 1771; US travel agency establishments fell about 45% from 1997 to 2013 as the GDS opened to consumers, while bookings kept growing. Hendel, Nevo and Ortalo-Magné give the mechanism directly: off-MLS prices are no lower, but off-MLS selling is slower and less likely to complete. The intermediary’s measurable value is matching, not price and not reach. Corrections: the travel decline is 45% (29,452 to 16,235 establishments), not the 59% widely repeated; overall P&C independent share slipped to 61.5% from 62.2%.

That working-capital float, rather than access, is the true binding constraint

The practitioner and economist lenses both argue the real ceiling is cash: brokers and forwarders do the work months before payment arrives. The documented case is real — Surge Transportation, roughly $150M gross revenue in 2022, filed Chapter 11 on 25 July 2023 owing about $12M to more than 5,000 carriers, with 16 of its top 20 creditors being factoring companies. But this is one firm, in US freight, and no causal study establishes float as the general constraint. The supporting staffing benchmarks came from vendor marketing and did not survive verification. Treat float as a real short-run constraint that can kill a solvent business, not as the answer to what caps the model.

03

The Hidden Connection

The academic lens says entry competes away any excess return until per-agent profit reaches zero. The historian says every intermediary that lost privileged access to the match was destroyed on schedule. Read separately, one is an equilibrium argument and the other is a story about technology.

They are the same statement. Entry is the mechanism by which anything replicable stops being valuable. Access to the match was simply the thing that could not be replicated — until MLS, the GDS and direct writers replicated it, at which point the protection vanished and the equilibrium asserted itself. This is why deal flow reads as the problem in every one of these episodes without ever being the cause: flow is the last visible thing to go.

Volume peaked immediately before each collapse. More travel was booked, more shares traded, more homes sold than ever — and the intermediaries read record volume as health and hired against it. Rising flow with falling per-deal economics is the terminal symptom of an access advantage already gone.

The assumption this briefing rests on (and the missing 6th lens)

Every lens reasoned about firms with a salesforce. Compass, eXp, NAR’s membership, Reagan’s benchmarks and both structural papers all describe multi-agent brokerages where entry, dilution and producer payroll are meaningful variables. The missing lens is the solo operator — the one-person insurance agent or two-person forwarder who is most of the Philippine SME market. For a firm with no agents, “entry dissipates rent” happens outside the business rather than inside it, and the internal constraint may simply be the owner’s own hours, which none of the five lenses modelled.

There is also no causal evidence from the Philippines at all. Every identified study is US real estate; insurance brokerage, freight forwarding and recruitment have descriptive work only. The regulatory environment differs materially, and one attempt to lean on Philippine statute in this very briefing produced three wrong claims. A Philippine-specific study could invert the conclusions without contradicting a single citation here.

04

What To Actually Do Differently

For an owner-operator of a Philippine commission business who believes their problem is not enough leads.

01
Produce the denominator before spending on leads.

Count inquiries that arrived 90 days ago and were never contacted a second time. “Deal flow is my constraint” is only a claim if you can state how many opportunities entered and what share closed. Without that number, a dead month is unmeasured rather than structural.

02
Watch revenue per deal, not deals.

Rising volume alongside falling per-deal economics is the pattern that preceded every historical collapse in this section. It reads as growth on the way down.

03
Name the thing a new entrant cannot copy this year.

If the honest answer is “nothing,” the equilibrium result applies to you and more leads will not change it. If it is a carrier appointment, a closed side of the market, or twenty years of referrals, that is the asset to defend and the reason tenure outearns effort tenfold.

04
Treat the cash gap as a separate problem with its own answer.

Float can end a business whose matching is fine. It is not the same constraint as access, it runs on a shorter clock, and it needs a financing answer rather than a strategy answer.

04b

What’s Safe to Assert

✓ Safe — verified against primary source

  • More agents entering does not raise sale probability or cut time-to-sale (Barwick & Pathak, RAND 2015).
  • When prices rise, brokers multiply, output per broker falls, real wages hold (Hsieh & Moretti, JPE 2003).
  • Off-MLS prices are no lower; MLS wins on speed and probability of sale (Hendel et al., AER 2009).
  • Compass paid 82.53% of Q4 2024 revenue out in commissions (SEC filing).
  • US independent agencies: 39.0% of personal lines, 87.2% of commercial lines, 2024.
  • SEC Rule 19b-3 ended 183 years of fixed commissions on 1 May 1975.
  • Philippine real-estate commissions are customary; RA 9646 sets no rate.

⚠ Say with a caveat

  • NAR income figures — attribute as a self-reported association survey.
  • The 40%/73%/23% entry figures — say “the authors’ model implies,” not “research shows.”
  • Post-settlement commissions — “returned to pre-settlement levels”; source is a market participant.
  • Philippine 2024 premiums of ₱440.39B — unaudited, 128 of 137 insurers, from the IC press release rather than the Key Data annex.
  • Licensed ordinary agents: 154,751 in 2024 (145,298 was 2023).
  • Working-capital float — real, documented in one bankruptcy, not established as the general constraint.

✕ Don’t assert

  • “RESA Section 29 caps a broker at 20 salespersons.” It is Section 32(a), and it is a minimum broker-to-salesperson ratio, not a cap.
  • “~15% of agents remain after four years.” The source illustrates ~9%, as a worked example rather than a finding.
  • “40% fill rate against 85% SLAs.” Vendor marketing; the 85% figure exists nowhere.
  • “35 brokerages vanished after May Day.” Untraceable to any primary source.
  • “Travel agencies fell 59% to under 10,000.” Census data give 45%, to 16,235.
  • ₱2M/₱5M recruitment escrow. It is ₱1M for both land- and sea-based.
  • “A dead month is a property of the model.” The evidence points the other way, and it functions as an alibi.
05

The Frontier Question

In a Philippine SME brokerage, what share of inquiries that arrive is never contacted a second time?

Every lens argued about what constrains these firms, and not one could cite the number that would settle it. If most arriving demand dies inside the business, the constraint is conversion and the framework should say so plainly. If almost all of it is worked and still does not close, access is the answer and the model needs defending rather than feeding. The number is cheap to measure and nobody measures it, because the pipeline lives in individual agents’ Messenger and Viber threads and disappears when they leave. That is also why the question has stayed open: the data is destroyed by the same behaviour that causes the problem.
