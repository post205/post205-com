01

The 60-Second Summary

All five lenses reject audience replacement, and one filing settles the premise: Coursera earned $290.6M of its $398.1M consumer revenue in 2024 from learners who had already registered — 73%, an 80% revenue retention. A business whose buyers are consumed by the sale cannot produce that number. The real problem is that nobody can evaluate teaching before buying it, and often not after. A résumé audit found for-profit health certificates drew 57% fewer callbacks for jobs with no licence requirement, and no significant penalty at all where the job required a licence — the same credential, judged differently depending on whether a third party vouched. The constraint is proof someone else will vouch for. The historian’s correction, which verification upheld, is that an operator cannot mint that proof alone: verification arrives as an industry institution, formed after a collapse, and the available move is to borrow a gatekeeper’s standing until one exists.

That Philippine licensing rules guarantee recurring demand

RA 10912 §10 does make continuing professional development a mandatory requirement for renewing a Professional Identification Card, and that is verified in the statute text. But PRC has extended acceptance of a CPD Undertaking — an affidavit in lieu of completed units — to 31 December 2026. So in practice a licensed professional can renew today without completing anything. Treat mandatory CPD as a structural opportunity, not a guaranteed revenue stream, and expect anyone in the room who holds a licence to know this better than you do.

03

The Hidden Connection

The first draft of this briefing argued that these businesses die when an independent party checks and publishes their outcomes. Verification was briefed to attack that claim, and falsified it.

Corinthian and ITT died of cash, not reputation: ITT’s board closed the company twelve days after the Department of Education barred new federally-aided enrolments and demanded a $247M letter of credit. Corinthian had traded for years after government reports, attorney-general actions and press exposure. The Flexner Report published outcomes, but the schools closed because state licensing boards and the profession stopped recognising their graduates. And International Correspondence Schools never died at all — the damning 1926 Carnegie study was followed by industry growth, and it survives today as Penn Foster.

Measurement is not the kill switch. A gatekeeper who controls money or licensure is, and measurement is what lets them pull it. Which is why a review centre advertises PRC results rather than its own: PRC controls the licence, so only PRC’s numbers carry weight.

The assumption this briefing rests on (and the missing 6th lens)

Every lens assumed the buyer wants an outcome that someone else will check — a passing rate, a job, a licence. The missing lens is the buyer who wants the experience itself: the hobby class, the parenting seminar, the faith-based course, the leisure workshop. For those, there is no third party to vouch and none is wanted, and the entire proof argument evaporates. That is a large share of what Philippine training providers actually sell, and no lens addressed it.

There is also no Philippine causal evidence here at all. The résumé audit is American, the administrative-data study is American, the MOOC data is from two US universities. What is genuinely local is regulatory and commercial: the Supreme Court decision, PRC’s publication practice, TESDA registration, and one review school’s price list.

04

What To Actually Do Differently

For a Philippine owner selling training, review or coaching who believes the problem is finding students.

01
Borrow a gatekeeper’s standing; do not try to mint proof yourself.

History says verification arrives as an institution, formed after a collapse, never built by one seller. The available move is smaller and older: a board result, a licence, a named employer who will testify, an accreditation you can qualify for. Find whoever controls the gate in your market and get them to vouch.

02
Measure your own outcomes before anyone forces you to.

Nobody audits review centres in this country. That is an opportunity rather than a shield: the provider who publishes a checkable number first, and keeps publishing it in bad years, is buying a position no marketing budget can replicate.

03
Count what share of revenue comes from people who already bought.

The premise that each buyer is consumed by the sale is false in general and testable in your business. If the share is near zero, the fix is a second thing to sell the same person, not more advertising.

04
Treat mandatory CPD as an opening, not an annuity.

It is a statutory requirement whose enforcement is currently suspended by an affidavit route running to the end of 2026. Build for the requirement, price for the reality.

05
If you teach for enjoyment rather than outcome, ignore most of this.

Where nobody wants a third party to certify the result, proof is not the constraint and the audience argument may well hold. Know which business you are in.

05

The Frontier Question

If one Philippine review centre published audited pass rates, would it win?

The whole argument predicts yes, and nobody has tested it. There is no regulator to compel disclosure, no PRC dataset by centre, and no competitive pressure to volunteer — so every provider advertises self-reported topnotchers and buyers have no way to compare. A single centre submitting to an independent audit would either capture the market or prove the theory wrong, and either outcome is worth more than another season of tarpaulins. The reason it has not happened is that the first mover bears the cost of a standard everyone else free-rides on, which is precisely why history says the institution forms after the collapse rather than before it.
